## Contract errors

Returned by the Soroban contracts themselves — `exchange-router`, `order-vault`, and the other bindings in [`packages/contracts/src/clients`](https://github.com/SO4-Markets/interface/tree/main/packages/contracts/src/clients) — when a call violates a contract-level rule.

## `INSUFFICIENT_COLLATERAL` {#insufficient-collateral}

**What you see:** "Insufficient collateral for this position."

**Likely cause:** The position's collateral is below what the market requires for the requested size and leverage.

**What to do:** Add collateral, or reduce position size/leverage, then resubmit.

## `INSUFFICIENT_LIQUIDITY` {#insufficient-liquidity}

**What you see:** "Insufficient liquidity for this trade."

**Likely cause:** The pool backing this market doesn't have enough available liquidity to take the other side of this trade right now.

**What to do:** Try a smaller size, or wait — available liquidity changes as other positions open and close.

## `INSUFFICIENT_OUTPUT_AMOUNT` {#insufficient-output-amount}

**What you see:** "Output amount is below your minimum."

**Likely cause:** The trade would have produced less output than the minimum you (or the client) specified, usually because the price moved between quote and execution.

**What to do:** Re-quote and resubmit, or widen your slippage tolerance if the move is expected.

## `INVALID_MARKET` {#invalid-market}

**What you see:** "This market is not available."

**Likely cause:** The market address or symbol in the request doesn't match a market this deployment knows about.

**What to do:** Confirm the market against /reference/contracts — an address from another network or an outdated build is the usual cause.

## `INVALID_ORDER` {#invalid-order}

**What you see:** "Order parameters are invalid."

**Likely cause:** One or more order parameters (size, price, trigger, or their combination) fail the contract's validation rules.

**What to do:** Check the specific field the wallet/contract call reports; a zero or negative size and an out-of-range trigger price are the most common causes.

## `INVALID_PRICE` {#invalid-price}

**What you see:** "Price is outside the allowed range."

**Likely cause:** The submitted or oracle price falls outside the band the contract allows for this operation.

**What to do:** Refresh the price and resubmit. If it persists, check /guides/troubleshooting#chart-not-loading — a stale oracle read produces this too.

## `LEVERAGE_TOO_HIGH` {#leverage-too-high}

**What you see:** "Leverage is above the maximum allowed for this market."

**Likely cause:** The requested position size relative to collateral exceeds the market's maximum leverage.

**What to do:** Reduce size or add collateral to bring effective leverage under the market's cap (see /concepts/margin-and-leverage).

## `MARKET_CLOSED` {#market-closed}

**What you see:** "This market is currently closed."

**Likely cause:** The market is temporarily paused (maintenance, oracle outage, or a deliberate freeze) and isn't accepting new operations.

**What to do:** Wait for the market to reopen. Existing positions are unaffected by a market being closed to new orders.

## `ORDER_NOT_FOUND` {#order-not-found}

**What you see:** "Order was not found or has already been executed."

**Likely cause:** The order ID no longer exists — it was already executed, cancelled, or never existed on this network.

**What to do:** Refresh your open orders list; if you expected it to still be pending, check whether it executed instead.

## `POSITION_NOT_FOUND` {#position-not-found}

**What you see:** "Position was not found."

**Likely cause:** The position ID no longer exists — it was already closed or liquidated, or belongs to a different account/network.

**What to do:** Refresh your positions list; check /guides/troubleshooting#position-not-appearing if you expected to see it.

## `SLIPPAGE_EXCEEDED` {#slippage-exceeded}

**What you see:** "Order failed because executed price exceeded your slippage tolerance. Increase tolerance or try again."

**Likely cause:** The price moved past your slippage tolerance between when the order was submitted and when it executed.

**What to do:** Increase slippage tolerance if the move is acceptable to you, or resubmit at the current price.

## `UNAUTHORIZED` {#unauthorized}

**What you see:** "You are not authorized to perform this action."

**Likely cause:** The signing account doesn't have permission for this action — often a mismatch between the connected wallet and the position/order owner.

**What to do:** Confirm you're signing with the same address that opened the position or placed the order.

## `ORDER_EXECUTION_FROZEN` {#order-execution-frozen}

**What you see:** "Order execution is temporarily paused. Your order has been saved and will execute when trading resumes."

**Likely cause:** Trading execution is paused network-wide, usually for a scheduled upgrade or an incident response.

**What to do:** No action needed — the order is saved and executes automatically once trading resumes. Check /resources/roadmap or the project's status channel for context.

## `CODE_ALREADY_TAKEN` {#code-already-taken}

**What you see:** "Code already taken"

**Likely cause:** The referral code you tried to create is already registered by another account.

**What to do:** Choose a different code.

## `CODE_NOT_FOUND` {#code-not-found}

**What you see:** "Referral code not found"

**Likely cause:** The referral code doesn't exist, or was mistyped.

**What to do:** Double-check the code, including case, and that it was entered on the correct network.

## RPC and network errors

Returned by the Stellar RPC node the transaction was submitted to, before or during inclusion in a ledger — these are about the transaction envelope or account state, not contract logic.

## `tx_bad_auth` {#tx-bad-auth}

**What you see:** "Transaction signature invalid. Try reconnecting your wallet."

**Likely cause:** The transaction's signature doesn't validate — often a stale session after switching accounts in the wallet extension.

**What to do:** Reconnect your wallet and resubmit.

## `tx_bad_auth_extra` {#tx-bad-auth-extra}

**What you see:** "Transaction contains unexpected signatures. Try reconnecting your wallet."

**Likely cause:** The transaction carries signatures the network didn't expect, which usually means it was built for a different transaction envelope.

**What to do:** Reconnect your wallet and resubmit; if it persists after a fresh connection, clear the wallet's cached session.

## `tx_bad_seq` {#tx-bad-seq}

**What you see:** "Transaction sequence is out of date. Refresh and try again."

**Likely cause:** The account's sequence number moved since the transaction was built — another transaction from the same account landed first.

**What to do:** Refresh and resubmit; the client will fetch the current sequence number.

## `tx_insufficient_balance` {#tx-insufficient-balance}

**What you see:** "Wallet balance is too low to submit this transaction."

**Likely cause:** The signing account doesn't have enough XLM (or the required asset) to cover the transaction.

**What to do:** Fund the wallet — see /guides/faucet on testnet — then resubmit.

## `tx_insufficient_fee` {#tx-insufficient-fee}

**What you see:** "Transaction fee is too low. Try again with a higher fee."

**Likely cause:** The submitted fee is below the network's current minimum, usually during congestion.

**What to do:** Resubmit; the client normally recalculates the fee automatically. Manually raise it if you built the transaction yourself.

## `tx_too_early` {#tx-too-early}

**What you see:** "Transaction submitted too early. Wait a moment and try again."

**Likely cause:** The transaction's time bounds start after the current ledger close time.

**What to do:** Rebuild and resubmit — this is a clock/time-bounds mismatch, not something to retry unchanged.

## `tx_too_late` {#tx-too-late}

**What you see:** "Transaction expired. Please try again."

**Likely cause:** The transaction's time bounds expired before it was included in a ledger — a slow signature, a stuck approval dialog, or network delay.

**What to do:** Resubmit. See /guides/troubleshooting#transaction-stuck-pending if this keeps happening.

## `op_exceeded_work_limit` {#op-exceeded-work-limit}

**What you see:** "Transaction too complex. Try a smaller position."

**Likely cause:** The operation needs more compute/resources than a single Soroban transaction is allowed to use, usually because the requested position or batch is very large.

**What to do:** Try a smaller size or split the operation into more than one transaction.

## `op_no_account` {#op-no-account}

**What you see:** "Destination account was not found."

**Likely cause:** The destination account referenced by the operation doesn't exist on this network yet.

**What to do:** Confirm the address and network; a new account needs an initial funding operation before it can be a destination.

## `op_underfunded` {#op-underfunded}

**What you see:** "Wallet balance is too low for this operation."

**Likely cause:** The account can't cover this specific operation's requirements, distinct from the overall transaction fee.

**What to do:** Fund the wallet — see /guides/faucet on testnet — then resubmit.

## Generic client-side buckets

When the error text doesn't match a specific code above but does match one of these patterns, `parseSorobanError` still returns a targeted message instead of the bare fallback.

## `simulation_failed` {#simulation-failed}

**What you see:** "Transaction simulation failed. Check the details and try again."

**Likely cause:** The RPC node couldn't simulate the transaction against current ledger state — often because it would fail on-chain for a reason not captured by a specific code above.

**What to do:** Check the transaction's contract call and arguments; if you can't tell why, include the raw RPC response in a bug report (see /guides/troubleshooting).

## `resource_or_budget` {#resource-or-budget}

**What you see:** "Transaction resources exceeded. Try a smaller position."

**Likely cause:** The transaction's CPU, memory, or ledger-entry footprint exceeds Soroban's per-transaction resource limits.

**What to do:** Reduce position size or split the operation across more than one transaction.

## `timeout_or_try_again` {#timeout-or-try-again}

**What you see:** "Network is busy. Please try again in a moment."

**Likely cause:** The RPC node is under load or a Horizon/RPC request timed out before completing.

**What to do:** Wait a few seconds and resubmit. If every request times out, the RPC endpoint itself may be degraded — see /guides/troubleshooting.

## `rejected_or_declined` {#rejected-or-declined}

**What you see:** "Transaction was rejected in your wallet."

**Likely cause:** You declined the signature request in your wallet extension, or closed its approval dialog.

**What to do:** No fix needed — resubmit and approve the request if you want the transaction to go through.

## Fallback {#unmatched}

**What you see:** "Transaction failed. Please try again."

**Likely cause:** The error text didn't match any known contract code, RPC code, or generic bucket above.

**What to do:** Resubmit once. If it keeps happening, report it — see "Reporting an error that isn't listed" below.

## Reporting an error that isn't listed

If you hit an error whose on-screen text isn't the fallback above but also doesn't match anything on this page, that's a gap in this reference, not necessarily a bug — [open an issue](https://github.com/SO4-Markets/interface/issues/new) with the exact on-screen text and, if you have it, the raw RPC/contract response. See [/guides/troubleshooting#collecting-information-for-a-bug-report](/guides/troubleshooting#collecting-information-for-a-bug-report) for what to include — and what never to include.
